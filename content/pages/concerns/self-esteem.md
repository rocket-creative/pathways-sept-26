# Self Esteem Therapy on Long Island

Low self esteem is a running inner commentary that says you are not enough, no matter what the evidence says. It is not a diagnosis, but it sits underneath many of the reasons people come to therapy. At Pathways Within, licensed therapists on Long Island help teens and adults change that commentary, in person in Nassau and Suffolk County and by telehealth.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Everyone doubts themselves sometimes. It becomes worth addressing when the doubt runs your decisions. People often tell us:

- You apologize for things that are not your fault.
- You turn down opportunities because you assume you will fail.
- Praise bounces off, and one piece of criticism sticks for weeks.
- You stay in relationships or jobs that treat you badly because you are not sure you deserve better.
- You compare yourself to everyone and always come up short.
- Your teenager has gone quiet, stopped trying at things they used to enjoy, or says things like "I'm stupid" or "nobody likes me."

Low self worth often travels with [anxiety](/concerns/anxiety) and [depression](/concerns/depression). It also shows up in people who look successful from the outside and feel like frauds on the inside. Your therapist looks at the whole picture, not just the label. ## How we treat self esteem at Pathways Within

Self esteem does not improve because someone tells you to think positively. It improves when you understand where the harsh voice came from, practice treating yourself differently, and collect real experiences that contradict the old story. Our therapists draw on the services below.

[Individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) is the core of most plans. Your therapist helps you trace the inner critic to its source, often a parent, a school, a relationship, or a period of bullying. You learn to notice the voice, question it, and answer it. Several of our clinicians list self esteem, confidence, or coping skills as specialties and use mindfulness and cognitive behavioral tools.

[Internal Family Systems (IFS)](/therapy/ifs) is a good fit for people whose inner critic feels like a separate character. IFS treats that critic as a part of you that took on a job long ago, usually to protect you. Your therapist helps you get to know that part instead of fighting it, which tends to soften it faster than arguing does.

[Teen therapy](/therapy/teen-therapy) meets adolescents where they are. Teen years are when self worth is most exposed, between social media, grades, and friendships that shift weekly. Our teen clinicians work with the teen directly and loop parents in on how to help at home without taking over.

[Group therapy](/therapy/group-therapy) offers something individual sessions cannot: proof, in real time, that other people do not see you the way you see yourself. Hearing others describe the same doubts, and being useful to them, changes the story faster than talking alone. ## What your first weeks look like

You or a parent contacts the Welcome Team. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. For teens, the intake includes the parent and the teen, with time for each. The team then matches you with a therapist who has openings and fits your age and needs.

Your first session is about your story and your goals. Expect your therapist to ask when the self doubt started and what it protects you from. By the third or fourth session, you have a few concrete things to practice, such as catching one critical thought a day and writing down what a fair friend would say instead. Progress is gradual. Most clients notice the voice getting quieter before it goes away. The full process is on [how it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

There is no medication for self esteem, and therapy is the treatment. When low self worth comes with depression or an anxiety disorder that makes it hard to function, medication can be part of the plan. Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, our psychiatric nurse practitioner, provides [medication management](/medication-management) for adolescents ages 10 and up and adults. Your prescriber and your therapist coordinate with your permission. For most clients on this page, therapy alone is enough.

## Common questions

### Is low self esteem a mental illness?

No. It is a pattern of thinking about yourself, and it is common. It is not in the diagnostic manuals as a condition on its own. It can be a symptom of depression or a result of trauma, and your therapist screens for both. You do not need a diagnosis to get help with it.

### How long does it take to build self esteem?

Longer than a few weeks, shorter than most people fear. Many clients notice a shift in how they talk to themselves within two to three months of weekly sessions. Lasting change usually takes longer, because you are collecting new experiences to replace old evidence. You and your therapist set the pace.

### My teen refuses to go. What can I do?

Start with the 360 intake yourself. The Welcome Team and a teen clinician can suggest how to raise it at home. Many teens who resist the idea go once, find it is not what they pictured, and keep going. Telehealth from their own room lowers the bar.

### What is the difference between self esteem and confidence?

Confidence is about a skill: you know you can do a thing. Self esteem is about worth: you believe you matter whether or not you do the thing. You can have one without the other. Therapy works on both, and [coaching](/coaching) can add structure for confidence in a specific area.

### Does insurance cover this?

Yes in most cases, because the work is billed as psychotherapy. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan.
