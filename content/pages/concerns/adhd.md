# ADHD Treatment on Long Island

ADHD treatment at Pathways Within is for adults, teens, and children who struggle to focus, finish, sit still, or slow down, and for the parents trying to help. Our licensed therapists and psychiatric nurse practitioner in Nassau and Suffolk County build one plan that can include therapy, skills, parent support, and medication. Care is in person at our Long Island offices or by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Everyone loses focus or acts on impulse sometimes. The National Institute of Mental Health describes ADHD as an ongoing pattern of inattention, hyperactivity, or impulsivity that interferes with daily life ([NIMH, ADHD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd)). Only a clinician can evaluate that. These are signs it is worth a conversation:

- Starting tasks feels impossible, and finishing them feels harder
- You lose track of time, keys, deadlines, or what someone just said
- Your mind jumps between thoughts and rarely settles
- You interrupt, blurt, or act before thinking, then regret it
- Your child's teacher keeps calling about focus or behavior
- Homework, bedtime, or mornings turn into daily battles at home
- You did fine in school but adult life feels like constant catching up
- You have been told you are lazy or careless and know that is not the whole story

## How we treat ADHD at Pathways Within

For adults and teens, [individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) focuses on what gets in the way day to day. Your therapist helps you build systems for time, tasks, and follow through, manage the frustration and low self esteem that often ride along with ADHD, and address anxiety or depression when they show up too. Ksusha Cascio, LCSW, our Chief Operating Officer, specializes in adult ADHD.

For children, [child therapy](/therapy/child-therapy) uses play and skills work matched to your child's age. Parents are part of every plan. For young children, [Parent Child Interaction Therapy](/therapy/pcit) coaches you in real time as you play with your child, so the tools work at home and not just in the office ([PCIT International](https://www.pcit.org/)).

Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, offers [medication management](/medication-management) for adolescents ages 10 and up and adults. Your prescriber reviews history, talks through options and side effects, and monitors how things go. Medication is never required, and your prescriber coordinates with your therapist when you give permission.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you or your child are experiencing and what you want to change. The team builds a plan and matches you with a therapist who works with your age group.

Your first session covers history, school or work, home life, and what a good outcome looks like. For children, the therapist usually meets with parents first. If an evaluation for medication makes sense, the Welcome Team schedules it with our prescriber. Within a few weeks you have a plan, a therapist, and a few practical tools already in use. Read the full process at [How it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

NIMH notes that ADHD is commonly treated with medication, psychotherapy, education or training, or a combination ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd)). Medication can help with focus and impulse control. Therapy and skills training help with the habits, relationships, and self image that medication does not touch.

At Pathways Within, you do not have to decide up front. Many adults start with therapy and add an evaluation later. Many families start with parent support and child therapy and consider medication only if school and home stay hard. Your therapist and your prescriber work from the same care plan.

## Common questions

### Do you diagnose ADHD?

Our therapists and prescriber assess for ADHD as part of your care. Some situations, such as school accommodations, call for formal psychological testing. [NEEDS: confirm whether formal ADHD testing is offered in house or referred out]. The Welcome Team can tell you what applies to you.

### Can adults be diagnosed for the first time?

Yes. Many adults recognize ADHD in themselves only after a child is diagnosed or after years of feeling behind. An evaluation looks at your history from childhood forward. Ksusha Cascio, LCSW, and several other clinicians work with adults who are figuring this out later in life.

### How young does treatment start?

Child therapy and Parent Child Interaction Therapy serve young children, with parents doing much of the work. Our prescriber sees adolescents ages 10 and up and adults. [NEEDS: confirm youngest age for child therapy].

### Is medication safe for my teenager?

Your prescriber reviews your teen's health history, discusses benefits and side effects with you and your teen, and monitors closely. You can ask every question you have before anything is prescribed. The decision is yours and your teen's.

### Does ADHD go away?

ADHD is generally a lifelong pattern, but how much it interferes can change a great deal with the right support. Most clients find that a mix of skills, structure, and sometimes medication makes daily life much more manageable.

### Does insurance cover ADHD treatment?

Most plans that cover therapy and psychiatric visits cover it. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake, and a limited number of sliding scale spots are available based on need and availability. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
