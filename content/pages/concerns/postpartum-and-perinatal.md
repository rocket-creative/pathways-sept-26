# Postpartum and Perinatal Mental Health Support on Long Island

Postpartum and perinatal support at Pathways Within is for people who are pregnant, trying to conceive, or in the first year or two after a birth and are not feeling the way they expected to. Our licensed therapists and psychiatric nurse practitioner in Nassau and Suffolk County help with anxiety, depression, overwhelm, and loss during this stretch. Care is in person at our Long Island offices or by telehealth, so you can talk from home while the baby naps.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Mood shifts in the days after birth are common and usually pass. When the low mood, fear, or numbness lasts for weeks, during pregnancy or after, it deserves attention. [NEEDS: citation for perinatal depression] Only a clinician can tell you what is going on. These are signs it is worth a conversation:

- You cry often and cannot say why
- You feel anxious about the baby constantly, even when everything is fine
- You cannot sleep even when the baby is sleeping
- You feel detached from the baby, or guilty that you do not feel what you expected
- Intrusive thoughts about something happening to the baby scare you
- You are irritable with your partner in a way that does not feel like you
- You feel like a failure as a parent or think your family would be better off without you
- Pregnancy after a loss has you braced for the worst every day

Partners can struggle too. Fathers and non birthing parents are welcome here.

## How we treat postpartum and perinatal concerns at Pathways Within

[Individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) is the center of care. Your therapist helps you sort what is exhaustion, what is anxiety or depression, and what is the ordinary shock of a new life. You work on sleep, intrusive thoughts, identity, and the relationship changes that come with a baby, at a pace that fits a newborn schedule. [NEEDS: confirm clinicians with perinatal training]

Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, offers [medication management](/medication-management). Your prescriber discusses medication safety during pregnancy and breastfeeding with you, reviews the options, and helps you weigh benefits and risks so the decision is informed and yours. She coordinates with your OB or midwife when you give permission.

Tia Baumohl, Certified Coach and Energy Medicine Practitioner, began her career supporting mothers and their partners through pregnancy and labor and has more than a decade of experience with this season of life. Her [coaching](/coaching) focuses on nervous system regulation, communication with your partner, and confidence. Coaching is not therapy, and it can be a good addition to it.

[Pregnancy massage](/wellness/massage) with our licensed massage therapists is available as coordinated care. Christine Cervo, LMT, is trained in prenatal massage. Massage is not a treatment for depression or anxiety, and it can help with the physical strain of pregnancy and recovery.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. Tell them your due date or your baby's age, and they will plan around it. The team matches you with a therapist and, if you want, our prescriber.

Your first session covers your pregnancy or birth story, sleep, support at home, and how you have been feeling. Your therapist checks on safety for you and the baby. Telehealth sessions from home are common in this stage, and a baby on your lap is fine. Read the full process at [How it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

Therapy alone helps many people through perinatal anxiety and depression. When symptoms are severe, or when you cannot sleep or eat enough to function, medication can be part of the plan. Some medications are considered compatible with pregnancy and breastfeeding, and your prescriber walks through what is known about each option.

At Pathways Within you can start with either and add the other. Your therapist can refer you to Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, at any point, and both providers work from the same care plan.

## Common questions

### Is it the baby blues or something more?

The blues usually lift within a couple of weeks. If low mood, anxiety, or numbness is lasting longer, getting worse, or making it hard to care for yourself or the baby, it is time to talk to someone. You do not have to decide which it is before calling.

### Can I take medication while breastfeeding?

That is a conversation for you and your prescriber. Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, discusses what is known about each option during pregnancy and breastfeeding so you can make an informed decision. Nothing is prescribed without that conversation.

### I am scared to say my intrusive thoughts out loud.

Scary thoughts about harm coming to the baby are common in new parents and do not mean you want it to happen. Therapists who work in this area hear them often. Saying them out loud is usually the moment they start to lose power.

### Can my partner come, or get their own support?

Yes to both. Partners can join sessions, and non birthing parents can have their own therapist. [Couples therapy](/therapy/couples-therapy) is also available when the strain has landed on the relationship.

### Do you help after a miscarriage or stillbirth?

Yes. Pregnancy loss is grief, and it often goes unacknowledged. Our therapists work with individuals and couples after loss and during pregnancy after loss. See also our [grief and loss](/concerns/grief-and-loss) page.

### Does insurance cover perinatal therapy?

Most plans that cover therapy cover it. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake, and a limited number of sliding scale spots are available based on need and availability. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).

## Take the next step

You do not have to wait until it is bad. Tell the Welcome Team where you are in pregnancy or how old your baby is and how you have been feeling. They will set up your 360 intake and match you with the right support, in person or by telehealth.
