# Anxiety Therapy on Long Island

Anxiety therapy at Pathways Within is for adults and teens whose worry has started running the day. Our licensed therapists in Nassau and Suffolk County help you understand what drives the fear, calm the body, and get back to the things you have been avoiding. Care is in person at our Long Island offices or by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Everyone feels anxious sometimes. The National Institute of Mental Health describes anxiety disorders as worry or fear that does not go away and can get worse over time ([NIMH, Anxiety Disorders](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders)). Only a clinician can tell you what is going on. These are signs it is worth a conversation:

- You feel tense, restless, or on edge most days, even when nothing is wrong
- Worry keeps you up at night or wakes you early
- You avoid places, people, or tasks because of how they make you feel
- Your heart races, your chest tightens, or your stomach turns before ordinary events
- You replay conversations or rehearse worst cases in your head
- Friends or family have said you seem stressed or short with them
- You have tried to manage it alone and it is not getting better

## How we treat anxiety at Pathways Within

Your care plan is built around you, not a single method. Most people start with [individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy). Your therapist helps you spot the thoughts and habits that feed the worry, practice skills that settle the body, and take small steps toward what you have been avoiding.

When anxiety has roots in an event or a period of your life, [EMDR](/therapy/emdr) can help the brain finish processing what got stuck. [IFS therapy](/therapy/ifs) helps you get to know the anxious part of you and why it works so hard. Many clients use one of these alongside weekly talk therapy.

Our psychiatric nurse practitioner offers [medication management](/medication-management) for adults and adolescents ages 10 and up. Medication is never required. Your prescriber talks through options, benefits, and side effects with you and stays in contact with your therapist when you give permission.

Some clients add [acupuncture](/wellness/acupuncture) with our licensed acupuncturist as a coordinated option. That can include the NADA protocol, an ear acupuncture approach used alongside mental health care. Acupuncture is not a substitute for therapy, and your care team coordinates it with your plan.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. You do not need to name a diagnosis or know what kind of therapy you want. The team builds a plan and matches you with a therapist who has openings.

Your first session covers your history, what anxiety looks like for you, and what a good outcome would be. Your therapist may give you one or two practical tools to use right away. By week three or four, most clients have a rhythm: weekly sessions, a few skills between them, and a clear sense of what you are working on. Read the full process at [How it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

NIMH notes that anxiety disorders are generally treated with psychotherapy, medication, or both ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders)). Therapy alone is the right starting point for many people. Others feel too flooded to use therapy skills until the body settles, and medication can help with that.

At Pathways Within, you do not have to choose in advance. Your therapist can refer you to our prescriber for an evaluation at any point. If you already take medication, she can review it. Both providers work from the same care plan, so you are not explaining yourself twice.

## Common questions

### How do I know if it is anxiety or just stress?

Stress usually has a cause and eases when the cause passes. Anxiety tends to stick around or move from one worry to the next. You do not need to sort that out before calling. Your therapist helps you figure out what is happening and what would help, and the 360 intake is a good place to start.

### Do you treat panic attacks?

Yes. Panic is one of the most common reasons people call us. Your therapist teaches you what is happening in the body during a panic attack, how to ride one out, and how to reduce how often they come. Medication management is available if panic is frequent or severe.

### Can you help my teenager?

Yes. Several of our therapists work with teens, and [teen therapy](/therapy/teen-therapy) has its own page. Our prescriber sees adolescents ages 10 and up. Parents are part of the plan, and your teen has private time with their therapist.

### How long does anxiety therapy take?

It depends on how long the anxiety has been present and what is driving it. Some clients feel a difference within a few sessions. Others work for several months. Your therapist reviews progress with you regularly so you always know where things stand.

### Does insurance cover anxiety therapy?

Most plans that cover therapy cover it. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).

### What if I am too anxious to make the call?

That is common. You can start with the form instead, and a Welcome Team member will reach out to you. There is no pressure to explain everything in the first conversation.
