# Stress and Burnout Therapy on Long Island

Stress is what happens when the demands on you outrun what you have to give. Burnout is what stress becomes when it goes on too long without relief. At Pathways Within, licensed therapists help adults who feel worn down, short tempered, or unable to switch off. We see clients in person in Nassau and Suffolk County and by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Everyone has hard weeks. Talking to a therapist makes sense when the hard weeks stop ending. Here is what people often tell us at the first call:

- You wake up tired and go to bed wired.
- Small problems feel like emergencies, and you snap at the people you love.
- You have stopped caring about work you used to take pride in.
- Headaches, jaw tension, stomach trouble, or a tight chest keep showing up, and your doctor cannot find a medical cause.
- You cannot remember the last time you relaxed without a screen or a drink.
- You keep saying "after this deadline," and the deadline keeps moving.

Long commutes, caregiving for aging parents, and the cost of living on Long Island add up. You do not need a diagnosis to get help carrying that weight. Long term stress can also shade into [anxiety](/concerns/anxiety) or [depression](/concerns/depression), and catching it early makes the work shorter. ## How we treat stress and burnout at Pathways Within

There is no single stress treatment. Your care plan depends on where stress lives for you: in your thoughts, in your body, or in your calendar. Most plans combine two or three of the options below.

[Individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) is the usual starting point. Your therapist helps you sort what you can change from what you can only carry differently. You practice ways to set limits, quiet the loop of worry, and rest without guilt. Several of our clinicians list stress management as a specialty.

[Coaching](/coaching) suits people who want structure around one specific goal, such as a boundary at work or a career decision. Coaching is not a substitute for therapy when stress has turned into depression or anxiety. The Welcome Team helps you decide which fits.

[Somatic therapy](/therapy/somatic-therapy) works with the body directly. Burnout often shows up as a nervous system that never gets the all clear. A somatic therapist teaches you to notice that state and bring it down.

[Massage](/wellness/massage) and [acupuncture](/wellness/acupuncture) are available at the same practice through the same Welcome Team. Our licensed massage therapists and licensed acupuncturist coordinate with your therapist, with your permission. The person working on your shoulders knows what you are working on in session. NCCIH summarizes what research does and does not show for [massage therapy](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/massage-therapy-what-you-need-to-know) and [acupuncture](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/acupuncture-effectiveness-and-safety).

## What your first weeks look like

Your first contact is with the Welcome Team, by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. It covers sleep, work, relationships, and your body, not just the word "stress."

From there, the team builds a care plan and matches you with a therapist who has openings. Your first therapy session is mostly listening. Your therapist asks what a good week would look like and what gets in the way. By the second or third session, you have one or two concrete things to try between visits.

Most people start weekly. Some add a massage or acupuncture visit in the same month. The Welcome Team stays your point of contact, so you never have to repeat your story to book something new. Read the full process at [how it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

Stress itself is not treated with medication. When long term stress has tipped into depression, anxiety, or sleep that will not come, medication may be part of the plan. Our psychiatric nurse practitioner offers [medication management](/medication-management) for adolescents ages 10 and up and adults. Your prescriber and your therapist coordinate with your permission.

Many people with burnout never need medication, and no one here pushes you toward it. If you are already taking something from another doctor, tell the Welcome Team so your plan accounts for it.

## Common questions

### Is burnout a mental health diagnosis?

No. Burnout is not a diagnosis in the manuals clinicians use in the United States, and you do not need one to start therapy. Your therapist treats what you describe: exhaustion, irritability, and loss of interest. If those symptoms point to depression or an anxiety disorder, your therapist tells you and adjusts the plan.

### How long does stress therapy take?

That depends on how long the stress has been building and what else is going on. Some clients feel steadier within six to eight weekly sessions. Others keep a standing appointment for months because it is the one hour that belongs to them. You and your therapist review progress together and decide.

### Do I have to quit my job or change my life to feel better?

No. Most clients keep the same job, family, and commute. Therapy changes how you hold the load, where you set limits, and how you recover. If a bigger change is right for you, your therapist helps you think it through, but that decision stays yours.

### Will insurance cover this?

Therapy for stress is billed as psychotherapy, which most plans that cover mental health include. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake. Massage and acupuncture coverage is more limited. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees) for details.
