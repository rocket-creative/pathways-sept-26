# Substance Use Treatment on Long Island

Substance use becomes a problem when it starts to cost you things, such as sleep, money, trust, or your sense of control, and you keep going anyway. At Pathways Within, licensed therapists and a psychiatric nurse practitioner help adults and teens on Long Island change their relationship with alcohol or drugs. We are an outpatient practice, not a detox or rehab program, and we say so up front.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

You do not have to hit a bottom, and you do not have to call yourself an addict, to talk to a therapist about drinking or using. People often reach out when:

- You set a limit for yourself and keep passing it.
- You are hiding how much you use, or lying about it to someone you care about.
- Mornings are getting harder, and you need something to get through them.
- A partner, parent, doctor, or employer has raised it with you.
- You use to fall asleep, to calm down, or to feel anything at all.
- You stopped once and it did not last, and you are not sure why.

Substance use often starts as a way to manage something else: [anxiety](/concerns/anxiety), [depression](/concerns/depression), trauma, or the aftermath of a deployment or a shift that went badly. Treating the substance use without treating that is why so many first attempts fail. [NEEDS: citation for "substance use disorders commonly occur alongside other mental health conditions"]

## How we treat substance use at Pathways Within

Our care is outpatient, which means you live at home, keep your job or school, and come in for scheduled sessions. That fits people whose use is a problem but who are medically safe to cut back or stop with support. If you need medical detox, a residential program, or intensive outpatient care, we will tell you honestly at intake and help you find it. The [SAMHSA National Helpline](https://www.samhsa.gov/find-help/national-helpline) at 1-800-662-4357 is free, confidential, and open 24 hours a day for treatment referral anywhere in the country.

[Individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) is where most clients start. Your therapist helps you understand what the substance does for you, what it costs, and what you want instead. Together you set a goal, whether that is stopping, cutting back, or deciding. You learn to handle cravings, triggers, and the situations where saying no is hardest. Several of our clinicians list addiction or substance use as a specialty.

[Group therapy](/therapy/group-therapy) adds people who understand. Groups reduce shame, which is the fuel most substance use runs on, and give you a place to practice honesty every week. [NEEDS: confirm whether a substance use or recovery group is currently running, and its format]

[Medication management](/medication-management) addresses the anxiety, depression, or sleep problems that often drive use. Your prescriber makes all medication decisions and coordinates with your therapist, with your permission.

Our [veterans and first responders program](/therapy/veterans-first-responders) is for people whose use is tied to service. Police, fire, EMS, and military clients often drink to sleep or to shut off what they have seen. Therapists in this program understand that culture. Veterans in crisis can dial 988 and press 1 or text 838255 to reach the [Veterans Crisis Line](https://www.veteranscrisisline.net/).

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are using, how much, what else is going on, and what you want to change. The team asks about safety and medical history to confirm outpatient care is the right level.

If it is, the team matches you with a therapist who has openings and, where it helps, schedules you with our prescriber. Your first therapy session sets a goal you actually agree with. Your therapist does not require abstinence to start. Sessions are usually weekly, and more often in the first month if you are stopping. Slips are treated as information, not failure. Read the full process at [how it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

Therapy is the core of outpatient substance use treatment. Medication often helps alongside it, especially for the depression, anxiety, or sleep problems that make sobriety hard to hold. Your prescriber evaluates you and explains any options. Some medications for alcohol or opioid use disorder require specific settings or monitoring; if what you need is outside what we offer, we refer you. ## Common questions

### Do I have to stop completely before I start therapy?

No. You can start while you are still using. Your therapist works with you on the goal you choose, and many clients move from cutting back toward stopping as they go. What we ask is honesty in the room about what you are using.

### Is this a rehab or an intensive outpatient program?

No. Pathways Within is a general outpatient practice. Sessions are usually once a week. If you need medical detox, residential care, or an intensive program with several sessions a week, we will say so at intake and help you find one. Many clients come to us after those programs for ongoing support.

### Will my employer or my family find out?

Not from us. Your treatment is confidential and protected by law. What you tell your therapist stays between you unless you are in danger. If you want a family member involved, you decide that. Teens have a separate conversation with their therapist about what parents will and will not hear.

### Can I get help for my drinking and my anxiety at the same time?

Yes, and you should. Anxiety, depression, and trauma are the most common reasons people use in the first place. Your therapist treats both. If medication would help the anxiety, the Welcome Team adds a visit with our prescriber to the same plan.

### Does insurance cover substance use therapy?

Most plans with mental health benefits cover outpatient therapy for substance use. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
