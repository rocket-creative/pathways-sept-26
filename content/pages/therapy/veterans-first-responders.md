# Therapy for Veterans and First Responders on Long Island

Therapy for veterans and first responders at Pathways Within is trauma focused counseling for people whose work put them in front of crisis, loss, and danger again and again. Licensed therapists trained in EMDR and trauma therapy offer it in person at our Nassau and Suffolk County offices and by telehealth. VA Community Care is accepted.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911. Veterans and their families can also reach the [Veterans Crisis Line](https://www.veteranscrisisline.net/) by dialing 988 and pressing 1, or by texting 838255.

## What this program is

Veterans and first responders hear words like strong, tough, and hero often, and with good reason. The trouble starts when you do not feel tough or strong, and the badge feels like a cage. Loneliness and loss that other people struggle to understand can make it hard to take part in life as a whole person.

This program pairs you with a therapist who has worked with service members, police, fire, EMS, dispatchers, nurses, and corrections staff. The tools are the ones with the strongest track record for trauma: [EMDR](/therapy/emdr), [trauma therapy](/therapy/trauma-therapy), and parts work. Some clients add [IET energy work](/wellness/energy-work/iet) or [massage](/wellness/massage) for the physical side of stress. Speaking up is not weak. Asking for help is the same strength you are known for.

## Who it helps

Our therapists work with veterans and first responders experiencing:

- [PTSD](/concerns/ptsd), nightmares, and being on guard all the time
- Anxiety or panic attacks
- [Depression](/concerns/depression) and feeling detached from family
- Trouble regulating mood or anger
- [Substance use](/concerns/substance-use) that started as a way to sleep or switch off
- Moral injury and guilt about calls or missions that did not go the way they should have

You do not need to name what you need before you call. That is part of our job. Trauma is not linear, and healing is not a straight line. Wherever you are, we can find a way forward together.

### For Veterans

The life of a service member is unlike other jobs. While you serve, frequent relocations and unusual obligations make it hard to build community outside the military. Once you leave, the loss of that community can leave you feeling ungrounded even when you finally have roots. Add years of stress and crisis exposure, and veterans carry a higher risk of depression, loneliness, and isolation long after their time is served.

Your therapist builds a plan around your history, your schedule, and what you want back. That may include talk therapy, EMDR, or IET, and it always includes a plan for handling what comes next.

### For First Responders

Crisis response is the job. Every shift puts you in front of once in a lifetime moments for other people, and each one leaves its own mark on you. You are not bad at your job if you are struggling to reconcile what you have seen with how you feel.

Leaving work at work is not an option for first responders. Your therapist helps you draw boundaries that let you find some peace at home and work through the trauma the job leaves behind. Whether you work a daily front line or respond to disasters, you are more than your job.

### When you are both

Many clients have held more than one high stress, crisis facing role. You may feel both defined by those roles and divided by them. Your therapist combines approaches into one plan that fits your whole history. You will have a voice in what you work on and how.

## What to expect in your first session at Pathways Within

Your first appointment is a conversation, not an interrogation. Your therapist asks what brought you in, what you have already tried, and what a good outcome looks like. You do not have to describe the worst call or deployment in detail on day one, or ever, if you choose EMDR.

Together you agree on a starting point and a pace. Many clients begin with grounding and sleep before touching trauma material. Sessions run about 50 minutes, weekly at first. Your therapist checks in on progress and adjusts the plan with you.

## How care works here

Every new client starts with a 360 intake with our Welcome Team. That conversation covers what you are experiencing across mind, body, and daily life, not only the one thing that made you call. The team then matches you with a therapist who knows this population.

When it helps, the plan can include [medication management](/medication-management) with our psychiatric nurse practitioner for sleep, panic, or mood. It can also include wellness services for the body. Everything is coordinated with your permission. Read more at [How it works](/how-it-works) or return to the [therapy hub](/therapy).

This program is available in person at all of our Long Island offices and by telehealth to clients in New York when a video visit is available. If you are not ready to sit across from someone yet, video is a fine place to start, and it keeps your care steady when work takes you elsewhere.

## Common questions

### Do I need a VA referral to be seen?

No. You can call us directly like any other client. If you want to use VA Community Care, the referral comes through the VA, and the Welcome Team can walk you through what to ask for. Many veterans use private insurance or self pay instead. See our [VA Community Care](/insurance/va-community-care) page for details.

### Will my department or command find out I am in therapy?

Not from us. Your records are confidential under federal and New York law, with narrow exceptions for immediate danger that your therapist explains at the first session. Nothing goes to an employer without your written consent.

### Do I have to talk about the worst things I have seen?

Not in the way you might fear. EMDR lets you process a memory without narrating it out loud. Other approaches build skills first and go at your pace. You set the limits, and your therapist respects them.

### Can my spouse or partner be involved?

Yes. Many clients add sessions with a partner once individual work is under way, and [couples therapy](/therapy/couples-therapy) is available. Family members carry the weight of this work too, and the Welcome Team can help them find support of their own.

### What if drinking or pills are part of how I cope?

Say so. Substance use is common in this population and it is not a reason to turn you away. Your therapist addresses it alongside the trauma. If a higher level of care is needed first, the team helps you find it. The [SAMHSA National Helpline](https://www.samhsa.gov/find-help/national-helpline) at 1-800-662-4357 is available around the clock.
