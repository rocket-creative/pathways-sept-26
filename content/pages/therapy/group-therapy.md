# Group Therapy on Long Island

Group therapy is a small, private circle of adults who meet with a licensed therapist to talk through shared struggles with others who understand. At Pathways Within, our therapists lead groups in person across Nassau and Suffolk County and, for some groups, by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## What group therapy is

Group therapy might sound scary or overwhelming at first. The reality is that we all want meaningful connection. Sitting in a room with people who are working on the same things is a way to share your concerns without feeling judged. You have more in common with other people than you think. As the weeks go by, you practice sharing your own feelings and listening to others, and you start to feel more confident and connected.

Group therapy is a special opportunity to join a safe space where a select group of people help each other talk through their problems. There is a professional in the room guiding the conversation and moving the group toward healing.

### Process oriented groups

Process oriented group therapy consists of no more than 10 people talking about their concerns and struggles with a therapist as the guiding force. These are called process groups because the goal is to help each other process feelings in healthy ways.

The format is not rigidly structured. Members are encouraged to bring up whatever they are dealing with at the time. The other members are unbiased strangers, so this is a chance to get honest feedback from people who are not being paid to tell you anything. Their words come from an authentic place.

Process groups usually run six to eight weeks. They are private and closed to the general public.

## Who it helps

While you are a unique person, your problems may not be unique. Many people struggle with issues like [anxiety](/concerns/anxiety) and [depression](/concerns/depression), and they want to talk about it with others who feel the same way. Our groups have served people facing:

- Anxiety and social anxiety
- Depression and isolation
- [Grief and loss](/concerns/grief-and-loss), including bereavement groups
- Low [self esteem](/concerns/self-esteem) and trouble speaking up
- [Stress and burnout](/concerns/stress-and-burnout)

One of the biggest worries people have about group therapy is not being able to speak up in front of others. That is the cliché first session, where most people are quiet and afraid of saying too much or not enough. Group therapy is excellent at making you aware of your feelings and giving you time to practice talking about them. Watching others find the courage to share often motivates the rest of the group. As you open up in a comfortable space, your confidence grows and strengthens your voice outside of therapy.

## What to expect in your first session at Pathways Within

Before you join a group, you meet with the group therapist one on one. That conversation confirms the group is a fit for what you are working on, and gives you a chance to ask questions. Your therapist explains the ground rules, including confidentiality.

In the first group session, the therapist introduces the format and everyone shares as much or as little as they want. No one is forced to speak. Most people say the awkwardness fades faster than they expected. Groups meet at a set time each week for the length of the group.

## How care works here

Every new client starts with a 360 intake with our Welcome Team. That conversation covers what you are experiencing across mind, body, and daily life. If a group fits, the team tells you what is running and when, and connects you with the group therapist. ## Common questions

### Is group therapy confidential?

Your privacy matters to us, and confidentiality is a major part of every group. We cannot guarantee that other members will keep that agreement, so use good sense when you share. Group therapy works best in an atmosphere of trust. When each member shares their story, they make themselves vulnerable. That often creates stronger bonds and makes members less likely to talk outside the group.

### Can I be in both individual and group therapy?

Of course. Some people find individual therapy more helpful, others prefer group, and a third set would rather do both. Individual and group therapy provide different benefits, so attending both is common.

### What if I am shy and cannot participate much?

Anxiety and stress are normal at first. New members usually see the benefit of sharing soon after joining, and the awkwardness of talking to strangers fades. Group therapy is a good environment to practice new skills and push yourself a little outside your comfort zone.

### What happens if I know someone in the group?

This happens from time to time. If you know someone in your group, tell your group therapist privately and we will discuss the best way forward. Sometimes both parties agree to stay in the group together. If either of you is not comfortable, we work together to find a solution.

### How do I find out what groups are running?

Contact the Welcome Team. Groups change through the year based on need and clinician availability, and the team keeps the current list. They can put you on a waitlist for a group that is not yet open.
