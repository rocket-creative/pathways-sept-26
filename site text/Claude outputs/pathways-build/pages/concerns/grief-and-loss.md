# Grief and Loss Therapy on Long Island

Grief therapy at Pathways Within is for adults and teens who have lost someone or something central to their life and are finding that time alone is not enough. Our licensed therapists in Nassau and Suffolk County give you a place to say what you cannot say anywhere else, and help you carry the loss without being crushed by it. Care is in person at our Long Island offices or by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Grief is not an illness. It is what love does when the person is gone. Still, some people get stuck in it, and some losses are too heavy to carry alone. Our founder, Rachel Lessard, LCSW-R, lost her father suddenly as a child and built this practice partly around what helped her. These are signs it is worth a conversation:

- Months have passed and the pain feels as raw as the first week
- You cannot stop replaying the death or the last conversation
- You avoid anything that reminds you of the person
- You feel guilty about what you did, did not do, or how you feel now
- You have stopped seeing friends or doing things you used to enjoy
- Sleep, appetite, or concentration have not come back
- People around you seem to think you should be over it
- The loss was sudden, violent, or involved a child, and no one wants to talk about it

Grief also follows losses that are not deaths: a divorce, a miscarriage, a diagnosis, a job, a home, a version of your life you expected to have. Those count here too.

## How we treat grief and loss at Pathways Within

[Grief therapy](/therapy/grief-therapy) is not about getting over it. Your therapist helps you make room for the loss, work through the parts that feel unfinished, and find a way to stay connected to the person while still living your life. There is no schedule and no right way to do it.

Many clients do this work through [individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy), where the sessions follow whatever the week brings. When grief has tangled with trauma, as it often does after a sudden or violent death, your therapist may bring in trauma focused approaches.

[Group therapy](/therapy/group-therapy) offers something one on one work cannot: other people who know. Sitting with people who have lost a spouse, a parent, or a child takes away the feeling that you have to explain or apologize. [NEEDS: confirm whether a grief specific group is currently running]

When grief has slid into depression that will not lift, our psychiatric nurse practitioner offers [medication management](/medication-management). Medication does not treat grief, and your prescriber will say so. It can help when sleep, appetite, and energy have collapsed for months.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are going through and what you need. You do not have to be composed. The team matches you with a therapist and, if it helps, a group.

Your first session is mostly listening. Your therapist wants to know who you lost, what happened, and what the days have been like since. There is no assignment and no timeline. Over the next few weeks you and your therapist find the places where the grief is stuck and start working there. Read the full process at [How it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

Therapy is the main treatment for grief. Most people do not need medication, and grief itself is not something to medicate away. When loss brings on a depression that lasts for months, or when sleep has broken down so badly that nothing else can improve, medication can help.

At Pathways Within your therapist can refer you to Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, for an evaluation if that point comes. She reviews your history and talks through options with you. Both providers work from the same care plan.

## Common questions

### How long should grief last before I get help?

There is no clock. Some people call in the first month because they cannot function. Others come a year or more later when everyone else has moved on and they have not. If you are wondering whether it is time, that is usually a sign it is.

### Is it normal to feel relief or anger after a death?

Yes. Relief after a long illness, anger at the person for leaving, or numbness instead of tears are all common. Grief is not one feeling. Your therapist will not judge any of it.

### Can you help with a miscarriage or pregnancy loss?

Yes. Pregnancy loss is grief, and it is often grief no one around you acknowledges. Our therapists work with individuals and couples after miscarriage, stillbirth, and infertility. See also our [postpartum and perinatal](/concerns/postpartum-and-perinatal) page.

### Do you work with children and teens who have lost a parent?

Yes. Several of our therapists specialize in children and teens, and [child therapy](/therapy/child-therapy) and [teen therapy](/therapy/teen-therapy) have their own pages. Parents are part of the plan.

### Does grief therapy mean I will forget the person?

No. The goal is the opposite. Good grief work helps you keep the person with you in a way that does not stop your life. Most clients say they feel closer, not further away, by the end.

### Does insurance cover grief therapy?

Most plans that cover therapy cover it. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake, and a limited number of sliding scale spots are available based on need and availability. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
