# LGBTQIA+ Affirming Therapy on Long Island

Affirming therapy means your therapist starts from one fact: your identity is not the problem you came in to solve. At Pathways Within, licensed therapists on Long Island work with lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, intersex, asexual, and questioning clients on the same things anyone brings to therapy, plus the things that come with living as yourself in a world that does not always make it easy. We see teens, adults, couples, and families in person and by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

You do not need a reason tied to your identity to see an affirming therapist. Many clients come for anxiety, a breakup, or a hard job, and simply want a clinician who will not make them explain themselves first. People also reach out when:

- You are figuring out your identity and want a place to think out loud without pressure.
- Coming out, or not coming out, to family or coworkers is taking up all your energy.
- You are in a relationship, or more than one, and want a therapist who understands its shape without judging it.
- Your teenager has told you who they are and you want to support them well.
- A past therapist, doctor, or faith community treated your identity as a disorder, and you are still carrying that.
- You feel unsafe or unwelcome in places that used to feel fine.

Ongoing stress from discrimination, rejection, or hiding takes a toll, and it shows up as [anxiety](/concerns/anxiety), [depression](/concerns/depression), and trouble sleeping. Your therapist treats the symptoms and takes the cause seriously. [NEEDS: citation for "minority stress is associated with higher rates of anxiety and depression among LGBTQ+ people"]

## How we treat LGBTQIA+ clients at Pathways Within

Affirming care is not a separate kind of therapy. It is a stance your therapist holds while using the same methods that help anyone. What is different is that you do not spend your sessions educating your therapist or bracing for a reaction. Our care plans draw on the services below.

[Individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) is the starting point for most adults. Your therapist works with you on whatever you bring: identity, relationships, family, work, trauma, or mood. Our Suffolk Clinical Director lists LGBTQ+ affirming care for teens and adults as a specialty.

[Couples therapy](/therapy/couples-therapy) is open to same sex couples, mixed orientation couples, and people in polyamorous or open relationships. One of our marriage and family therapists lists polyamorous relationships as a specialty. Your therapist works with the relationship you actually have, not a template.

[Family therapy](/therapy/family-therapy) helps when a family member has come out and the household is adjusting. Parents often want to do the right thing and are not sure what it is. A family therapist gives everyone a place to say what they are afraid of and learn what support looks like in practice.

[Teen therapy](/therapy/teen-therapy) gives adolescents a confidential space with a clinician who is affirming by default. Parents are part of the plan, and your teen's therapist explains what stays private and what does not before the first session. We do not offer or refer for any practice that tries to change a person's orientation or gender identity.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. You can share your pronouns and the name you use at that point, and the team records them so you do not have to correct anyone later. [NEEDS: confirm intake form has pronoun and chosen name fields]

The team matches you with a therapist who lists affirming care as a specialty and has openings. Your first session covers your story and your goals. If identity is not what you came in for, your therapist follows your lead. If it is, you set the pace. Read the full process at [how it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

Most clients on this page need therapy only. When depression, anxiety, or sleep problems make daily life hard, medication can help alongside therapy. Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, our psychiatric nurse practitioner, provides [medication management](/medication-management) for adolescents ages 10 and up and adults. Your prescriber and your therapist coordinate with your permission.

## Common questions

### What does "affirming" actually mean in the room?

It means your therapist accepts your orientation and gender identity as facts about you, not symptoms. They use your name and pronouns, understand the vocabulary, and do not need you to justify your relationships. If something comes up they do not know, they ask rather than assume.

### Are all your therapists affirming?

Every clinician at Pathways Within is expected to treat every client with respect. The clinicians listed on this page have specific training or focus in LGBTQIA+ care, and the Welcome Team matches you with one of them when you ask. [NEEDS: confirm practice wide affirming care training or policy statement]

### Can my partner and I come together if we are not monogamous?

Yes. Our couples therapists work with polyamorous and open relationships. You decide who is in the room. Some clients come as a pair and add sessions with other partners. Your therapist helps you figure out the structure that fits.

### My teen just came out. Should they see a therapist?

Only if they want to or are struggling. Coming out is not a problem to treat. If your teen is anxious, withdrawn, or facing rejection at school, a teen therapist can help. Family therapy can also help you and your partner sort out your own reactions without putting that on your child.

### Does insurance cover affirming therapy?

Yes, in the same way it covers any psychotherapy. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake, and a limited number of sliding scale spots are available based on need and availability. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
