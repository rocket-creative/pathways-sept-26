# Therapy for Relationship Issues on Long Island

Therapy for relationship issues at Pathways Within is for couples, families, and individuals who keep having the same fight, or who have stopped fighting and stopped talking. Our licensed therapists in Nassau and Suffolk County help you understand the pattern, say what you actually mean, and decide together what comes next. Care is in person at our Long Island offices or by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Every relationship has rough stretches. Therapy helps when the rough stretch has become the whole relationship, or when one of you is carrying a pattern from one relationship to the next. These are signs it is worth a conversation:

- You have the same argument on a loop and it never resolves
- One of you shuts down and the other pushes harder
- Trust has been broken and you do not know how to rebuild it
- You feel more like roommates or coworkers than partners
- A parent and a teenager cannot be in the same room without a blowup
- A divorce, a move, a new baby, or a blended family has changed everything
- You keep choosing partners who hurt you and want to understand why
- You want to know whether to stay or go, and you cannot think clearly about it

## How we treat relationship issues at Pathways Within

[Couples therapy](/therapy/couples-therapy) puts both of you in the room with a therapist who stays neutral. Our founder, Rachel Lessard, LCSW-R, works with couples using the Gottman Method, and several licensed marriage and family therapists on the team specialize in couples work. You learn to notice the cycle you fall into, slow it down, and reach each other underneath it.

[Family therapy](/therapy/family-therapy) works the same way with parents, kids, siblings, or the whole household. It is a good fit when a teenager is struggling, when co parenting after divorce is not working, or when a blended family is finding its footing.

[Individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) is for the person who wants to work on their own side of the pattern, whether their partner is willing to come or not. That can mean attachment patterns, codependency, anger, boundaries, or figuring out why the same thing keeps happening. Several of our clinicians work with LGBTQIA+ clients and polyamorous relationships.

[Coaching](/coaching) with Tia Baumohl, Certified Coach and Energy Medicine Practitioner, is a different kind of support. It focuses on communication, assertiveness, and attachment patterns in a forward looking, skills based format. Coaching is not therapy, and Tia works with individuals and couples who want that structure.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what is happening and what you want to change. If you are coming as a couple or family, the team asks who will attend and matches you with a therapist trained in that work.

In the first session, your therapist hears from everyone and learns the history of the relationship. Some therapists then meet with each person once on their own. By the third or fourth session you have a clear picture of the pattern and a plan for working on it. Read the full process at [How it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

Relationship issues are treated with therapy, not medication. When anxiety, depression, ADHD, or trauma in one partner is driving the conflict, treating that condition often helps the relationship too. Our psychiatric nurse practitioner offers [medication management](/medication-management) for those conditions, and your couples or family therapist can refer you when it makes sense. Both providers work from the same care plan.

## Common questions

### My partner will not come. Can I still do this?

Yes. Individual therapy on your side of the relationship changes the pattern more than most people expect. Many partners join later once they see something shift.

### Does couples therapy mean we are headed for a breakup?

No. Most couples who come here want to stay together and do not know how to get back to each other. Some come to decide, and the therapist supports that too. Your therapist does not push either outcome.

### Do you work with LGBTQIA+ couples and nontraditional relationships?

Yes. Several of our clinicians are LGBTQIA+ affirming and work with polyamorous and other nontraditional relationships. See our [LGBTQIA+ affirming therapy](/concerns/lgbtqia-affirming-therapy) page.

### What is the difference between coaching and couples therapy?

Therapy treats patterns rooted in history, mental health, and emotion, and is provided by a licensed clinician. Coaching is a structured, skills based process focused on communication and goals. The Welcome Team helps you decide which fits, and some clients use both.

### Can we do family therapy with a teenager who refuses?

Often, yes. Your therapist may start with the parents, then invite the teen in once there is something concrete to offer. [Teen therapy](/therapy/teen-therapy) is also available on its own.

### Does insurance cover couples or family therapy?

Some plans do and some do not. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake, and a limited number of sliding scale spots are available based on need and availability. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
