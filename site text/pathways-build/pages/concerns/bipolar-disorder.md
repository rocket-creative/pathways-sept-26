# Bipolar Disorder Treatment on Long Island

Bipolar disorder is a mood condition marked by periods of unusually high energy or irritability and periods of depression, with stretches of stability in between. It is treatable, and most people do best with medication and therapy together. At Pathways Within, a psychiatric nurse practitioner and licensed therapists provide both under one roof, in person on Long Island and by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Only a qualified clinician can diagnose bipolar disorder, and a web page cannot. These are the kinds of things people describe when they reach out to us:

- Days or weeks of needing very little sleep and feeling unstoppable, followed by a crash.
- Spending, driving, or decisions during those stretches that you would not make otherwise.
- Depression that keeps coming back even after a period of feeling fine.
- Family members telling you that you seem like a different person at times.
- A previous diagnosis of depression and antidepressants that made things feel worse or more wired.
- A family history of bipolar disorder and a growing sense that something similar is happening to you.

Bipolar disorder is often mistaken for [depression](/concerns/depression) at first, because people tend to seek help during the low periods. A careful evaluation asks about the high periods too. [NEEDS: citation for bipolar disorder]

## How we treat bipolar disorder at Pathways Within

Bipolar disorder is usually managed with medication plus therapy, and that is how we approach it here. Medication treats the biology of mood episodes. Therapy helps you recognize early warning signs, protect your sleep, and repair what episodes may have damaged. Your prescriber coordinates with your therapist, with your permission, so the two halves of your plan stay in step.

[Medication management](/medication-management) is central. Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, MSN, BSN, RN-BC, is our psychiatric nurse practitioner. She works with adolescents ages 10 and up and adults. Your first visit with her is an evaluation. She reviews your history, sleep, past medications, and what has helped or hurt. If medication is appropriate, she explains the options and what to expect, and she follows up regularly to adjust. Your prescriber makes all medication decisions. Your therapist does not.

[Individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy) gives you a place to learn your own pattern. Your therapist helps you track mood and sleep, spot the first signs of an episode, and build a plan for what to do when you see them. Therapy also treats what often travels with bipolar disorder: anxiety, shame after an episode, and strain at work or school. Several of our clinicians, including our Nassau Clinical Director, list bipolar disorder as a specialty.

[Family therapy](/therapy/family-therapy) helps the people who live with you understand the condition and their role in it. A family therapist helps everyone agree on what support looks like and what to do in an emergency.

When bipolar disorder is complicated by alcohol or drug use, which is common, the Welcome Team can add support from our [substance use](/concerns/substance-use) services to the same plan.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. If you already have a diagnosis or a current prescription, tell the team so your plan starts from there.

Because medication is usually part of the plan, the team schedules you with our prescriber early, often in the same weeks as your first therapy session. Expect the evaluation with your prescriber to be thorough and to include questions about your family history and past episodes. Your therapist, meanwhile, starts with your story and a simple daily log of sleep and mood.

Follow up visits with your prescriber happen more often at first, then spread out as things settle. Therapy is usually weekly. The Welcome Team stays your single point of contact for both. Read the full process at [how it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

For bipolar disorder, the honest answer is both. Therapy alone is rarely enough to prevent mood episodes, and medication alone leaves you without the skills to catch an episode early or rebuild afterward. [NEEDS: citation for "bipolar disorder is typically treated with a combination of medication and psychotherapy"]

If you are already seeing a psychiatrist elsewhere and want therapy here, that works. Your therapist coordinates with your outside prescriber, with your permission. If you are seeing a therapist elsewhere and want medication management here, that works too. Tell the Welcome Team at intake.

## Common questions

### Can you diagnose bipolar disorder at Pathways Within?

Yes. Our psychiatric nurse practitioner and licensed therapists can evaluate for bipolar disorder. An evaluation takes more than one conversation and includes your history, sleep, family background, and any past treatment. If you have been told you have depression and wonder whether it is something more, say so at intake.

### Will I have to take medication forever?

That is a decision for you and your prescriber, revisited over time. Many people with bipolar disorder stay on medication long term because it prevents episodes. Some adjust or reduce under supervision. Stopping on your own raises the risk of relapse, so the conversation always goes through your prescriber.

### Can I do therapy without medication?

You can start there, and some people do while they decide. Your therapist will be honest with you about what therapy can and cannot do for bipolar disorder on its own. If your therapist thinks an evaluation with the prescriber would help, they will say so, and the choice remains yours.

### Do you treat teens with bipolar disorder?

Yes. Tiffany Roberts sees adolescents ages 10 and up, and several of our therapists specialize in teens. Diagnosis in adolescents is done carefully, since mood swings are also part of normal development. Parents are part of the plan.

### Is treatment covered by insurance?

Both medication management and therapy are usually covered by plans that include mental health benefits. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
