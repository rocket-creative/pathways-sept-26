# Trauma and PTSD Treatment on Long Island

Trauma and PTSD treatment at Pathways Within is for adults who are still living inside something that happened, whether it was one event or years of them. Our licensed therapists in Nassau and Suffolk County offer EMDR, IFS, somatic therapy, and a dedicated program for veterans and first responders. Care is in person at our Long Island offices or by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Feeling shaken after something frightening is normal, and most people recover with time. The National Institute of Mental Health describes PTSD as a condition that can develop after a shocking, scary, or dangerous event, when the fear and stress continue long after the danger has passed ([NIMH, PTSD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd)). Only a clinician can tell you what is going on. These are signs it is worth a conversation:

- Memories, nightmares, or flashbacks keep pulling you back
- You avoid places, people, or conversations that remind you of it
- You are always on alert, startle easily, or cannot relax
- Sleep is broken, and you are exhausted and irritable
- You feel numb, detached, or cut off from people you love
- You blame yourself or feel like the world is no longer safe
- You drink or use more than you used to, just to get through
- It has been months or years, and it is not fading

## How we treat trauma and PTSD at Pathways Within

There is no single trauma treatment here, because no two histories are the same. Your care plan draws on the approaches below, in whatever combination fits you.

[Trauma therapy](/therapy/trauma-therapy) is the foundation. Your therapist helps you understand how trauma affects the brain and body, builds skills for calming the alarm system, and works through what happened at a pace you control. Several of our clinicians specialize in complex PTSD, sexual assault, domestic violence, and relationship trauma.

[EMDR](/therapy/emdr) helps the brain finish processing memories that got stuck, without requiring you to retell every detail ([EMDR International Association](https://www.emdria.org/about-emdr-therapy/)). [IFS therapy](/therapy/ifs) helps you get to know the parts of you that carry the pain and the parts that protect you from it. [Somatic therapy](/therapy/somatic-therapy), including Somatic Experiencing with Lauren Hollander, LCSW, SEP, works with the body directly, where trauma often lives long after the mind has moved on.

Our [veterans and first responders program](/therapy/veterans-first-responders) is built for people whose trauma came from the job. Several clinicians, including our founder, specialize in this work and understand the culture, the repeated exposure, and the reasons it is hard to ask for help.

Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, offers [medication management](/medication-management) for adults and adolescents ages 10 and up. Your prescriber can address sleep, nightmares, panic, or depression that come with PTSD and coordinates with your therapist when you give permission.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. You do not need to describe the trauma in that call. The team matches you with a therapist trained in the approach that fits.

The first sessions are about safety and stability, not the event itself. Your therapist learns your history, what triggers you, and what supports you have. You practice grounding skills you can use right away. Processing the trauma begins only when you both agree you are ready. Read the full process at [How it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

NIMH notes that PTSD is treated with psychotherapy, medication, or both ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd)). Trauma focused therapy is the core of treatment for most people. Medication can help with the symptoms that make therapy hard to use, like severe insomnia or constant panic.

At Pathways Within you can start with either and add the other. Your therapist can refer you to our prescriber at any point, and both providers work from the same care plan.

## Common questions

### Do I have to talk about what happened?

Not in detail, and not before you are ready. EMDR and somatic approaches in particular do not require you to narrate the event. Your therapist will never push you into a memory you have not chosen to work on.

### Can I have PTSD from something that happened years ago?

Yes. Symptoms can begin months or years after an event and can last for years without treatment. Many of our clients are working on things that happened in childhood or early in a career.

### I am a first responder. Will my therapist understand the job?

Several of our clinicians specialize in veterans and first responders and have worked with police, fire, EMS, and military clients for years. They know the culture and the stigma. Read more on the [veterans and first responders](/therapy/veterans-first-responders) page.

### What if I also drink or use to cope?

That is common with trauma, and it does not disqualify you from care. Your therapist addresses both together, and the Welcome Team can add support for [substance use](/concerns/substance-use) to your plan.

### How long does trauma treatment take?

For a single event, some clients see real change in a few months. For long standing or repeated trauma, plan on longer, steady work. Your therapist reviews progress with you so you always know where things stand.

### Does insurance cover trauma therapy?

Most plans that cover therapy cover it, and we work with VA Community Care Network. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake, and a limited number of sliding scale spots are available based on need and availability. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
