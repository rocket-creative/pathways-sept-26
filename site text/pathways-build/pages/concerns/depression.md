# Depression Therapy on Long Island

Depression therapy at Pathways Within is for adults and teens who have felt low, empty, or exhausted for weeks and cannot shake it. Our licensed therapists in Nassau and Suffolk County help you understand what is weighing on you, rebuild energy and routine, and reconnect with the people and things that matter. Care is in person at our Long Island offices or by telehealth.

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Veterans: press 1) or call 911.

## Signs it may be time to talk to someone

Sadness comes and goes. The National Institute of Mental Health describes depression as a mood disorder that affects how you feel, think, and handle daily activities, with symptoms present most of the day for at least two weeks ([NIMH, Depression](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/depression)). Only a clinician can tell you what is going on. These are signs it is worth a conversation:

- You feel sad, empty, or numb most days
- Things you used to enjoy feel like chores or feel like nothing
- You sleep too much or too little, and either way you wake up tired
- Your appetite has changed without trying
- It is hard to focus, decide, or finish simple tasks
- You feel guilty, worthless, or like a burden
- You have pulled away from friends and family
- You have thoughts that life is not worth it

If that last one is true today, please call or text 988 before you read further.

## How we treat depression at Pathways Within

Most people start with [individual therapy](/therapy/individual-therapy). Your therapist helps you notice the thought patterns that keep the mood low, rebuild small routines that restore energy, and work through whatever loss, stress, or history sits underneath. Sessions are practical and paced to what you can manage.

Our psychiatric nurse practitioner offers [medication management](/medication-management) for adults and adolescents ages 10 and up. Your prescriber talks through options, benefits, and side effects with you and coordinates with your therapist when you give permission.

For adults whose depression has not responded to several other treatments, we offer [ketamine assisted therapy](/therapy/ketamine-assisted-therapy), provided with your prescriber and therapist working together.

[Group therapy](/therapy/group-therapy) is another option. Depression tells you that you are alone in this. Sitting with other people who understand is one of the fastest ways to prove it wrong.

## What your first weeks look like

You contact the Welcome Team by form, call, or text. A team member schedules your 360 intake, a conversation about what you are experiencing and what you want to change. The team builds a plan across therapy, medication management, and wellness as needed and matches you with a therapist who has openings.

Your first session covers your history, how the depression shows up day to day, and what feeling better would look like for you. Your therapist checks on safety, sleep, and support at home. In the following weeks, you set a few small, concrete goals and start working on them together. Read the full process at [How it works](/how-it-works).

## Medication, therapy, or both

NIMH notes that depression is usually treated with medication, psychotherapy, or a combination of the two ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/depression)). For mild to moderate depression, therapy alone is often enough. When the mood is heavy enough that getting to a session feels impossible, medication can lift things enough for therapy to work.

At Pathways Within you can start with either and add the other. Your therapist can refer you to Tiffany Roberts, PMHNP, for an evaluation at any point. If you already take an antidepressant, she can review whether it is still the right fit. Both providers work from the same care plan.

## Common questions

### How is depression different from feeling sad?

Sadness has a cause and lifts with time. Depression lingers for weeks, drains energy and interest, and often shows up without a clear reason. You do not have to decide which one you have before calling. That is part of what the 360 intake and your first session are for.

### Will I have to take medication?

No. Medication is one option, and many clients do well with therapy alone. If you and your therapist think it could help, you can meet with our prescriber for an evaluation. The decision stays with you.

### What is ketamine assisted therapy and who is it for?

It is a treatment for adults with depression that has not improved after other approaches. Your prescriber screens for medical fit, and a therapist works with you during and after sessions. It is not a first step. Read more on the [ketamine assisted therapy](/therapy/ketamine-assisted-therapy) page.

### Do you work with teens who are depressed?

Yes. Several of our therapists specialize in adolescents, and [teen therapy](/therapy/teen-therapy) has its own page. Our prescriber sees ages 10 and up. Parents are part of the plan, and your teen has private time with their therapist.

### How long does treatment take?

It varies. Some clients notice a shift within a few weeks. For long standing depression, several months of steady work is common. Your therapist reviews progress with you so you always know where things stand.

### Does insurance cover depression therapy?

Most plans that cover therapy cover it. Coverage varies by provider, service, and plan. The Welcome Team verifies benefits after your 360 intake, and a limited number of sliding scale spots are available based on need and availability. See [insurance and fees](/insurance-and-fees).
