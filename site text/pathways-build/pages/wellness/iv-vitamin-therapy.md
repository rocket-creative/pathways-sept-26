# IV Vitamin Therapy on Long Island

IV vitamin therapy delivers fluids and vitamins directly into a vein through a small catheter, administered by a registered nurse after a health screening. At Pathways Within, we offer IV hydration and vitamin infusions to adults who have been screened and cleared for the service at our Long Island offices. ## What IV vitamin therapy is

An IV infusion places a thin, flexible catheter into a vein, usually in the arm, and delivers a sterile solution over a set period. The solution is typically a base of fluids for hydration, with vitamins or minerals added according to the menu the nurse and client select. Because the infusion bypasses digestion, the contents enter the bloodstream directly.

At Pathways Within, a registered nurse administers every infusion. The nurse reviews your health history, checks your vital signs, confirms the infusion is appropriate for you, and stays with you during the session. Infusions are prepared according to standing orders. [NEEDS: name of supervising prescriber or medical director for standing orders]

IV vitamin therapy is a wellness service. It is not a treatment for any illness, and we do not make claims about energy, immunity, mental clarity, or recovery from drinking. If you have symptoms that concern you, see your physician.

## Who it helps

Clients ask about IV therapy for general hydration and as a wellness routine alongside other services here. Whether it is appropriate for you depends entirely on the screening. Some clients pair an infusion with [massage](/wellness/massage) or [cryotherapy](/wellness/cryotherapy) on the same visit.

If you came in because of fatigue tied to [stress and burnout](/concerns/stress-and-burnout) or an ongoing [chronic illness](/concerns/chronic-pain-and-illness), the Welcome Team may suggest starting with therapy or a conversation with your physician rather than an infusion. IV therapy does not address the causes of fatigue.

## Who it is not for

IV vitamin therapy is not for everyone, and the nurse may decline to proceed after screening. Infusions are generally not appropriate for people who are pregnant or breastfeeding, who have kidney or heart disease, or who have had an allergic reaction to any infusion ingredient. Tell the nurse about every medication and supplement you take, any recent hospital stay, and any diagnosis you have received. If you feel unwell on the day of your appointment, we reschedule. When in doubt, ask your physician before booking.

## What to expect in your first session at Pathways Within

Your first visit begins with a screening. The registered nurse reviews a health questionnaire with you, asks about medications and allergies, and takes your blood pressure, pulse, and temperature. If anything raises a concern, the nurse pauses the visit and may ask you to check with your physician first.

If you are cleared, you choose an infusion from the current menu with the nurse's guidance. [NEEDS: IV menu with ingredients and volumes] The nurse cleans the site, places the catheter, and starts the drip. You sit in a reclining chair for the duration, usually 30 to 60 minutes. [NEEDS: confirm infusion duration] You can read, rest, or talk.

After the infusion, the nurse removes the catheter, applies a small bandage, and checks how you feel before you leave. Mild bruising at the site is common. Tell us right away about any swelling, pain, rash, or shortness of breath during or after the session.

## Common questions

### Who administers the infusion?

A registered nurse administers every infusion and stays with you during the session. The nurse completes your screening, places the IV, monitors you, and removes the catheter at the end. [NEEDS: RN name and credential]

### Do I need a screening every time?

Your full health questionnaire and vital signs are reviewed at your first visit. At later visits the nurse rechecks your vital signs and asks whether anything has changed, including new medications or diagnoses. If something has changed, the nurse may ask you to check with your physician before continuing.

### Does it hurt?

You feel a brief pinch when the catheter is placed. After that, most people feel little or nothing beyond a cool sensation in the arm. Tell the nurse if you feel pain, burning, or swelling at the site, and the infusion will be stopped and checked.

### Can I do IV therapy if I take medication?

Sometimes. Some medications and conditions interact with infusion ingredients, so the nurse reviews everything you take before proceeding. Bring a list of your medications and supplements to your first visit. When there is any question, ask your physician first.

### Is IV vitamin therapy a substitute for eating well or seeing a doctor?

No. IV therapy does not replace a balanced diet, water, sleep, or medical care. If you have ongoing fatigue, frequent illness, or other symptoms, see your physician. IV therapy at Pathways Within is a wellness service for adults who have been screened and want it as part of their routine.
